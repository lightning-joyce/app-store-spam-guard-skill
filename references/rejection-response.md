# Responding to a spam or repetitive-content action

Use only after reading the exact review message and current policy.

## Immediate response

1. Pause resubmission.
2. Preserve the message, build, metadata, screenshots, notes, ID, and timeline.
3. Classify: sibling duplication, third-party template similarity, saturated concept, insufficient value, metadata/ownership, or white-label account structure.
4. Audit the broader catalog.
5. Distinguish a request for information from a demand for material changes. Follow the actual next-step instruction; if told not to resubmit and to answer questions, prepare answers first.
6. Recommend clarification, merge, withdrawal, material redesign, ownership clarification, or an evidence-based appeal as appropriate. Diagnosis alone does not authorize execution.

## Not a material remedy

New icon/name/colors/screenshots, keyword rewrites, a new bundle/package ID, another account, obfuscation, symbol renaming, dependency churn, arbitrary refactoring, one token feature, or citations to approved competitors.

## Apple response

Answer all information requests before considering appeal. If ambiguity remains, recommend a specific clarification question or review call. Do not appeal a request for additional documentation. After clarification fails to resolve a supported factual dispute, an appeal can explain the exact guideline, concern, comparisons, test steps, rights, and separate-app rationale. Follow current official appeal instructions; do not appeal in parallel with a corrected resubmission.

### Combined 4.3 / 4.2.6 questionnaire

An observed review message requested the following nine subjects. This is a preparation checklist, not a claim that every app must submit the same questionnaire. Use the exact order and wording of the current message when replying:

1. Concrete functionality and problem solved, not marketing copy.
2. Specific target users and usage context.
3. Substantive market gap or improvement over available alternatives, with supported comparisons.
4. Actual pre-submission beta testing, real feedback, and changes made. If none, say so; label later testing with its actual dates.
5. Standalone versus suite, and substantive differences from each relevant suite app.
6. Whether this could be a feature/IAP/content in an existing app and the user-facing reason for separation.
7. Significant code, frameworks, or assets shared with sibling apps beyond standard frameworks, identifying apps and each component's role.
8. Third-party shared codebases, SDKs, templates, or content libraries beyond standard frameworks, identifying origins and original added functionality.
9. Client/partner involvement, concept/brand/content ownership, and why the submitting account is appropriate.

Map every answer to evidence or a clearly stated unknown. Use [evidence-gates.md](evidence-gates.md) for provenance and user-validation discipline. Do not fill missing facts with plausible narrative or imply that more polished notes remedy a repetitive product. Avoid copying private app IDs, names, user data, or correspondence into reusable skill materials.

## Google Play response

Distinguish rejection, removal, suspension, and termination. Fix all identified and adjacent issues before resubmission, including affected tracks. Google permits one appeal per enforcement action. If the app is repetitive, redesign or aggregate it instead of appealing wording; premature resubmissions can escalate.
