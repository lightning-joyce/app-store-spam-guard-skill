# Responding to a spam or repetitive-content action

Use only after reading the exact review message and current policy.

## Immediate response

1. Pause resubmission.
2. Preserve the message, build, metadata, screenshots, notes, ID, and timeline.
3. Classify: sibling duplication, third-party template similarity, saturated concept, insufficient value, metadata/ownership, or white-label account structure.
4. Audit the broader catalog.
5. Choose merge, withdraw, material redesign, ownership clarification, or appeal of a factual error.

## Not a material remedy

New icon/name/colors/screenshots, keyword rewrites, a new bundle/package ID, another account, obfuscation, symbol renaming, dependency churn, arbitrary refactoring, one token feature, or citations to approved competitors.

## Apple response

Ask a concise factual question in App Store Connect when ambiguous. Appeal when evidence shows a factual error or a materially redesigned product was misunderstood. Include the exact guideline, reviewer concern, sibling/template comparison, test steps, ownership/licenses, separate-app rationale, and material changes. Apple staff guidance recommends answering information requests first and filing one appeal per rejection.

## Google Play response

Distinguish rejection, removal, suspension, and termination. Fix all identified and adjacent issues before resubmission, including affected tracks. Google permits one appeal per enforcement action. If the app is repetitive, redesign or aggregate it instead of appealing wording; premature resubmissions can escalate.
